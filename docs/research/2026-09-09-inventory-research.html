How to run inventory across a warehouse and six service vans

What working HVAC contractors actually do, what ServiceTitan's inventory module can and can't carry, the software landscape, and a recommended operating model for the distributor → warehouse → van flow.

Compiled 2026-09-09 · ~120 searches across trade press, ServiceTitan docs & podcasts, GitHub, YouTube, distributor programs · Reddit and HVAC-Talk block automated reading; their threads are cited from search snippets only.
Bottom line
  1. Process beats software. Every contractor who made this work did the same four things: named one owner, made logging materials on the invoice the only way to get restocked, restocked each van from a labeled tote pulled by the warehouse, and kept vans to a core 50–150 SKU template with min/max on every bin.
  2. Your flow is exactly the textbook model: two min/max loops. Van below min → warehouse transfer. Warehouse below min → distributor PO. Consumption comes off the invoice.
  3. ServiceTitan should be the system of record. Its Inventory module is already in your plan and matches the model precisely — but it's a one-way switch, needs Purchasing and a clean pricebook first, and its counting app is the weakest part. Nobody who abandoned it lacked features; they lacked an owner.
  4. There's no open-source "van stock" product worth adopting. If you buy beyond ST, Ply is the only tool built for HVAC trucks with native ST sync. Sortly/inFlow are generic and don't talk to ST.
  5. The scanner app we built has a clear role: bin labels, opening counts, and weekly cycle counts that post into ST as adjustments — not a parallel system of record. Align its codes to ST pricebook codes so one label works everywhere.

1. What contractors actually do

The pattern is remarkably consistent from 6-truck shops to 100-truck fleets. Sources are named contractors on the record, not vendor blogs.

Templates with min/max per van type

Service vans and install vans get different lists. Katy Plumbing (30 techs) ties every truck to min/max levels; Service America cut trucks from 315 → 150–175 SKUs and $20K → $14K per truck once usage data existed.

The warehouse restocks, not the tech

DiFilippo's: GM pulls used parts from a locked cage into per-tech bins, up to 3×/day in summer. Katy: warehouse manager fills a named bin per tech every night. Ace Hardware Home Services budgets one hire per location for purchasing + inventory.

Invoice logging is the only trigger

"We wouldn't allow them to use any other way to order their parts." — Katy Plumbing. The #1 rollout failure everywhere: techs not listing every material on the invoice. Fix it with process, then software.

Count when the vans are in

Evenings or a closed day; weekday counts get skewed by jobs closing afterward. Weekly top-20 usage + top-10 value items, monthly rotation, quarterly dead-stock review. Offload anything unused in 6 months.

Bins first, software second

Number and label every warehouse bin and van shelf before turning anything on. Min/max written on the bin label. Color by category; "hot zone" at the side door for top movers.

Keep techs out of supply houses

A supply run is 1–1.5 hrs. Katy Plumbing put it at $1–2M/yr of unbilled time across 30 techs; Nigel Mulgrew: a $2.50 fitting costs $100–150 in tech time. Kennihan gained +1 call/tech/day.

2. The replenishment model

Two min/max loops chained together. The invoice is the consumption event that drives both.

Loop A — Warehouse → van

  • Trigger: van available + on-order < min → replenish to max.
  • Mechanism: warehouse picks into a tote labeled with the van ID; tech confirms receipt on mobile (that receipt is the sign-off).
  • Cadence: daily for high performers, weekly minimum (Fri PM or Mon AM).
  • Rule: if it moved, it was transferred. Van-to-van swaps without a transfer are the #1 source of drift.

Loop B — Distributor → warehouse

  • Trigger: transfers deplete the warehouse below its min → PO.
  • Rhythm that works: order Wed, receive Fri, vans full Mon.
  • Lever: distributor programs — Johnstone Truck Stock / PartStock, R.E. Michel ESP, Ferguson kitting — deliver to the shop tagged by van and can substitute for part of a warehouse hire.
  • Later: VMI or consignment for the low-value consumable tail once the process is stable (shrink fell 10–15% → 3–5% in the cases found).

3. ServiceTitan: fit, gotchas, decision rule

Fit is excellent. The Inventory module is built around exactly this: locations (warehouse, one truck per tech), templates with min/max per item per location type, replenishment records generated when available + on-order drops below min, bulk creation of transfers (warehouse → truck) or POs (vendor → warehouse), receiving that posts quantities, cycle/full counts, adjustments with GL mapping, and free Full Procurement integrations with Johnstone, Ferguson, R.E. Michel, Winsupply and others that push contractor pricing into the pricebook and send POs electronically. It is included in your plan (not a separately priced add-on) but must be activated by your CSM, and Purchasing must be live first.

One-way door. "If you turn on Inventory, there is no turning back" — disabling leaves duplicated pricebook items and orphaned truck templates. Serialized inventory is separately irreversible. Do the prerequisites first: clean pricebook (vendor, cost, part # on every tracked item), every purchase through a PO for 30–60 days, a named owner, a Saturday opening count with tracking start Sunday.

Where it's strong keep

  • Invoice → van decrement → replenishment, no double entry
  • Job costing lands on the right job
  • Free distributor P2P (pricing + e-PO)
  • Scales from 5 trucks to 200 (A1 Garage)

Where it's weak watch

  • Inventory mobile app: 3.2★; counts against On Hand not Available, so staged parts read negative
  • No unit-of-measure in the app (rolls → feet)
  • Batched transfers can leave stuck negatives
  • Counter buys inflate truck stock unless consumed on the invoice
  • QuickBooks inventory and ST truck replenishment can't run simultaneously; QBO adjustments don't export

Decision rule

If Rocket will name an owner and enforce invoice-line discipline: run native ST Inventory (ST Mobile for consumption, Inventory app for receive/pick), and bolt on a lighter count tool only if the ST app proves painful by month two. If that discipline is uncertain: run Purchasing only in ST plus a scanning-app count cycle whose variances post as weekly bulk adjustments — you keep costing right without committing to the irreversible switch.

Recommendation: don't build a parallel system of record. Point the scanner app at the jobs ST does worst — bin/label printing, opening counts, weekly van cycle counts — and post its variances into ST as bulk adjustments (ST supports XLSX adjustment import). Key its items by the ST pricebook code so a single Code 128 label serves ST Mobile, the ST Inventory app, and the scanner.

5. Methods playbook

Min / max (par)

Min = avg daily use × lead-time days + safety stock. Max = min + order qty. Safety stock ≈ (max daily use − avg) × max lead time. Van lead time = "next restock" (≈1 week + buffer); warehouse lead time = distributor's real lead time. Compute per truck from the last 60–90 days of invoice data; keep heating and cooling season sets; review quarterly.

Two-bin / kanban

For cheap fast movers (wire nuts, fuses, straps, common caps/contactors): two labeled bins, work from one, the empty bin is the reorder signal. Physical backstop that survives a missed invoice line. Not for expensive or long-lead parts.

ABC classes

A (motors, compressors, refrigerant, boards, thermostats): count weekly/monthly, 99% target. B: quarterly, 98%. C: 1–2×/yr, 96% — and the two-bin candidates.

Cycle counting

Consistency beats volume. Trucks: 1–2 vans per week so each is fully counted every 3–6 weeks. Warehouse: A items weekly + ~20 rotating bins; one annual full count for the books. Every adjustment gets a reason code.

Kitting

Install kits, maintenance kits, hard-start kits, fan-motor kits in labeled totes: one pick, fewer missing parts. ST has no kit item type — model as a Service with attached materials, or a warehouse pick template. Review kit contents quarterly against real usage; distributors (Ferguson, Johnstone "install boxes") will kit too.

Labels

Label locations first (WH-A-03-02, VAN04-S2-B7), items second. Code 128 for bins and internal item codes; manufacturer UPC where it exists; QR only when you need to encode more. Thermal-transfer polyester labels — thermal paper fades in a hot van.

6. Recommended operating model for Rocket Cooling

Roles

  • Parts / warehouse coordinator (≈0.5 FTE at 6 vans) — receiving by scan, POs, weekly van restock into totes, warehouse cycle counts, variance investigation. The single most predictive success factor.
  • Service manager — weekly truck inspection (with the count), tech accountability, incentives for clean stocked trucks.
  • Techs — every material on the invoice before job close; unused parts to a marked returns bin; confirm transfers on mobile; keep two-bin signals honest.

Phases

Phase 1
weeks 1–4

Process + physical (no ST switch yet)

  • Name the owner. Write the one-page rule: nothing moves without an invoice line, transfer, or PO.
  • Build two van templates (Service, Install) of 50–150 core SKUs with min/max from 60–90 days of ST invoice usage; a Warehouse template.
  • Bin and label the warehouse and all six vans in Code 128 (the labels we started printing).
  • Opening counts of all seven locations using the scanner app.
Phase 2
days 30–60

ServiceTitan Purchasing live

  • Every purchase through an ST PO; receiving posts quantities.
  • Turn on Johnstone / Ferguson / R.E. Michel Full Procurement for pricing and e-POs; scheduled delivery to the shop, will-call only for emergencies.
  • Clean the pricebook: vendor, cost, part # on every tracked item. Reconcile to QuickBooks.
Phase 3
day 60+

ServiceTitan Inventory on

  • One warehouse, six trucks sourced from Default Warehouse, templates loaded, "Only Replenish Max" set, Serialized off.
  • Saturday beginning-inventory count → tracking start Sunday.
  • Weekly replenishment by bulk transfer into van totes; tech confirms on mobile.
  • Counts in the scanner app (or ST's app if it proves fine) → weekly bulk adjustments with reason codes. Evaluate Ply at month 3 only if counting stays painful.

Metrics to track

  • Inventory accuracy per location (≥95% overall; 99 / 98 / 96 by A / B / C)
  • Parts runs per tech per week (target → 0) and first-time-fix rate
  • Shrink as % of COGS per van (<2–3% monthly; 10% is the ceiling in the literature)
  • Inventory value per van and turns; days from consumption to restock; unrecorded-transfer count; warehouse variance $ by month
